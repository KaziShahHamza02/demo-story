# Episode 01: The Boy Who Lived

Mr. and Mrs. Dursley, of number four, Privet Drive, were proud to say that they were perfectly normal, thank you very much. They were the last people you would expect to be involved in anything strange or mysterious, because they just didn't hold with such nonsense.

Yet on the first Tuesday of November, the ordinary world quietly cracked at the seams. Owls swooped past bedroom windows in broad daylight, cloaked strangers whispered excitedly on street corners, and a tabby cat sat motionless on the Dursleys' garden wall, reading a street sign as if it were a map.

By midnight, Privet Drive was silent—until a tall, thin man with a silver beard long enough to tuck into his belt appeared out of nowhere. Albus Dumbledore clicked a silver Put-Outer twelve times, plunging the street into darkness, before turning to the tabby cat.

"I should have known that you would be here, Professor McGonagall," Dumbledore said softly.

The cat transformed into a severe-looking woman in emerald robes. Her voice trembled as she spoke of the rumors sweeping the wizarding world: that You-Know-Who was gone, and that Lily and James Potter were dead—yet their infant son, Harry, had somehow survived.

"Is it true?" McGonagall whispered. "After all he did... all the people he killed... he couldn't kill a little boy?"

"We can only guess," Dumbledore replied gently. "We may never know."

A low rumble broke the stillness. High above the clouds, a speck of light grew into a roaring flying motorbike. Rubeus Hagrid—twice as tall as a normal man and nearly five times as wide—landed gently on the pavement, cradling a bundle of blankets in his massive arms.

Inside the bundle slept a baby boy with a tuft of jet-black hair and a curious, lightning-bolt-shaped cut on his forehead.

"Had no trouble, did you, Hagrid?" Dumbledore asked.

"No, sir—house was almost destroyed, but I got him out all right before the Muggles started swarmin' around," Hagrid sniffled, wiping his eyes on his moleskin coat.

Dumbledore took the sleeping child and laid him carefully on the cold doorstep of number four, tucking a sealed letter into the blankets. He knew that Harry would grow up away from fame, away from magic, until he was old enough to bear the weight of his own name.

"Good luck, Harry," Dumbledore murmured, turning on his heel as the streetlamps flickered back to life.

Behind the locked oak door, the Dursleys slept undisturbed—completely unaware that ten years from now, the boy on their doormat would receive a letter that neither lock nor distance could stop.
